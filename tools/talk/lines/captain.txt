## captain.chat
I've kept the Deepwatch Log longer than any of my wardens have been on the watch.
The Log is older than the post. The first pages are in a hand nobody can read anymore.
My wardens are young. They should be. The road wears you out.
I don't ask for brave wardens. I ask for careful ones. Brave ones go down stairs.
The Deepgate was here before the walls. We only built the post to keep it company.
The lantern on our banner was carved over the Deepgate long before the Watch came. We borrowed it.
People think the hard part of a captaincy is giving orders. It's writing names on the barracks wall.
I read the old entries some nights. Same monsters, same road, same fear. It comforts me, oddly.
The Guild sends its thanks down the road in barrels of flour. I've never had kinder letters.
Lamp lit, road watched, gate shut. Three things. Do them every watch and the world above stays whole.
I don't mind the dark. It's honest about itself.
My wardens grumble about the stew. A post that grumbles is a post that's alive.
Sit with me a while. Most days nobody speaks to me except to report.
The lever beside the Deepgate has a lock on it. I carry the key. I'd rather carry anything else.
Every captain of this post has stood where I stand and looked at that door. Every one of us wondered.

## captain.past
Twelve of us went down the Long Stair, years ago. Five came back up. I was the youngest.
We were sent down to learn how deep it goes. We never learned. It goes on.
Our Log entry for that patrol is four words long. "The stair goes on." I wrote them.
I won't tell you what's down there. Not because it's secret. Because I'd have to remember it properly.
I was a quarry hand up top, before the Watch. I liked the sky. I still think about it.
The captain before me was one of the five as well. Gone now. That leaves me.
I swore the oath young. "I do not go down." I broke that line once, on orders. Never again, orders or no.
The night we came back up, the post had kept every brazier burning for us. They weren't sure we were still us.
I've buried more wardens than I've trained. No. That's not true. It only feels true.
When I took the captaincy, the old captain gave me the key and said one word: "Don't." That was all.

## captain.work
I read every line my wardens write in the Log. Every one. Then I add my own.
Each watch I try the Deepgate bar myself. My wardens check it too. I check their checking.
The rule is mine to keep: no one passes the Deepgate. Not lords, not Guild masters, not friends.
Drills, patrols, supplies, letters. Captaincy is mostly counting, {player}. Counting and listening.
I send a patrol down the road every third day. Out to the last torch, a little past, then home.
The key to the gate-lever hangs on a chain round my neck. It's lighter than it looks. Heavier than it feels.
When the Guild asks to dig near the post, I say no. When they ask why, I point at the Deepgate.
I teach my wardens to fear the right things. The dark you can't see into, and the bravery you can't take back.
Each season I send a runner to the Guild camps. "Cave roads quiet," the message says. I make sure it's true first.

## captain.news
The Log has had three quiet watches in a row. I've learned not to trust that.
The supply cart's due within the month. We're down to our last barrel of arrows and our last good joke.
One of my archers saw torchlight on the far road where no camp should be. I've sent word to the Guild.
The Deepgate's hinges were cold to the touch this morning. Colder than the stone. I've written it down.
Word from the other posts: all quiet along the ways down. For now.
My wardens turned back a band of rattlers at the bend last night. No one hurt. That's the news I like.

## captain.heart
I count the five of us who came back. Then I count the seven who didn't. Every night.
Sometimes I think the Deepgate is waiting for me. Not angry. Patient.
If I fall, the key goes to whichever warden is steadiest. I've already chosen. I haven't told them.
I want my wardens to grow old above ground. That's all. That's the whole of my ambition.
You walk in and out of the dark as if it were nothing, {player}. I envy that.
Some days the weight of this post is nothing. Some days it's the whole mountain.
When my watch is truly over, I want to sit by a river and listen to water that isn't dripping.

## captain.adventure
Explore if you must. Go sideways, not down. Down is where the stair is, and you've no business there.
If you find the Hush, the black sculk places, turn back. I know what hunts there. I've no wish to meet it.
Take a friend if you can. Twelve of us went down together, and still it wasn't enough. One alone is less.
The Old Delvers left halls all through this rock. Read their carvings. They knew things we've forgotten.
Come back by the road when you're done. I like to write "returned" in the Log.
Mark your route, carry milk for the cave spiders, and never trust the silence in a big cave.

## captain.night
I don't sleep much. The Log is good company at night, and it doesn't snore.
At night I walk the wall and lay my hand on the Deepgate. Cold iron. Still shut. Good.
The dark watch was always mine, even as a young warden. I hear things better at night.
Go and sleep, {player}. The Watch is awake. That's what we're for.
