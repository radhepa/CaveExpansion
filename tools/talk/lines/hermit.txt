# The hermit of {place} (e.g. "Old Moss Hollow"), a mossy mushroom-grove cave. Old, gentle, odd, wise, a little lonely; talks
# to the mushrooms; calls the visitor "surface-walker" (now and then "topsider"). Gender-neutral: no pronouns for the hermit,
# Tamsin or Pell. Once a Guild delver, a better one than most, pick-partner of Tamsin (who taught everyone to sound a roof) at
# the camp now called the Buried Seam. The shift robbed the pillars for their ore; Tamsin said the roof was "singing wrong" and
# nobody listened. The day it fell the hermit was up top fetching lamp oil, and Tamsin was working the hermit's turn at the face.
# Nine days of digging, then the Guild sealed the drift; the hermit stayed below ("somebody ought to stay where they can hear
# them") and stopped counting at a hundred shifts (about thirty years). Went back to the Seam once, saw lamps moving in the
# rubble, never again; brought back a small lantern, maybe young Pell's lost first lantern. Knew Old Hesk (owes six coppers).
# The giant red mushroom is "the Bishop", the brown one "Old Thatch"; small ones are named for the lost shift (Tamsin, Pell,
# Bramble, Wick). Mudge is the axolotl in the pond. Brews night-sight: "Gold I can dig. Carrots I have to beg." Own saying:
# "Slow is a kind of light." Uses the Guild greeting "Light to you" / "And the roof over you" and the Guild sayings.
# The Watch once came to see if the hermit was a monster and left with soup. The Deep Dark is "the Hush", its hunter "the Listener".

## hermit.chat
Mushrooms like the dark and a bit of damp, and they don't like being rushed. Very like me.
That's the Bishop, the big red one. Old Thatch is the brown. They've been arguing for twenty years.
The little mushrooms each have a name. Bramble, Wick, Tamsin, Pell... I named them for the shift.
Glow berries! Pick one if you like. They taste of a little sun, gone a bit sleepy.
The pond's fed by a drip from the roof. One drop at a time, since long before me. That's patience.
Mudge lives in the pond. Pink, frilly, smiling all the time. I've never seen anything so content.
I talk to the mushrooms. They don't talk back, exactly. But they listen better than most folk ever did.
Moss grows on anything that sits still long enough. I've had to shake it off my own boots.
Slow is a kind of light. You'll see more by going slow than any torch will show you.
Those roots hanging down come from a tree up top. An azalea, I think. It's been reaching for me for years.
Bats are good company. They come and go and they never ask how you are.
I don't miss much from up top. Bread. I miss bread. And people laughing in another room.
A cave is never truly silent. Water ticks, stone creaks, gravel whispers. You learn the words.
The Guild taught me to mind the roof. The hollow taught me to mind the rest.
I keep a lamp lit in the crawl, in case anyone ever needs to find their way in. And now someone has.
Spiders don't come in here. I think the mushrooms frighten them. Or the smell of my stew.
Mushroom stew, mushroom bread, mushroom tea. I am at least half mushroom by now, I think.
Never eat a mushroom you haven't been introduced to.
Clay from the pond bank makes good pots. Lopsided ones. Those are the ones I make.
Up top they think caves are empty. Caves are full! You only have to sit still long enough to see it.
I had a cat once, up top. Grey as slate. I wonder sometimes what became of it.
The stone's warmer here than anywhere else I found in the deep. Maybe that's why I stayed.
The Watch came by once, years ago, to see if I was a monster. I gave them soup. They decided no.
I hum the old Guild songs to the mushrooms. They grow better. Don't laugh. It works.
The glowing lumps up in the caps? Shroomlight. Warm as a hearth and twice as quiet.
Everything down here eats something else. Except the moss. The moss eats patience.
My hands still remember the pick. They itch when I hear stone crack.
I counted days by notches. Then I counted shifts. Then I stopped counting and started living.
That big leaf by the pond tips you off if you stand on it. Don't. It's Mudge's.
A good cave smells of wet stone and green things. A bad one smells of nothing at all. Leave the bad ones.
I sweep the hollow every morning. Mostly I move the dust from one side to the other. It's a kind of order.
Have you seen the purple caves, deep down, that ring like bells? I went once. Sat a whole day just listening.
Light behind you, light ahead. I say it every time I fetch water, even if it's ten steps.
Funny thing, being alone. You become very good company for yourself. Not always polite company.

## hermit.chat.new
Don't be shy. I'm the strangest thing in this cave, and I'm quite harmless.
First time in a hollow? Touch whatever you like. Except the red one. The Bishop's ticklish.
You'll get used to the glow. And the smell. And me. Mostly in that order.

## hermit.chat.close
You know, {player}, you're the first friend I've made since the roof came down. I'd forgotten how.
When I'm gone, somebody ought to look after the mushrooms. I'd like it to be you.
I've told you more than I've told anyone in thirty years. The mushrooms are jealous.

## hermit.past
I was Guild once. Twenty shifts on the pick, maybe more. Good hands. Bad luck.
Tamsin and I worked the same face for six shifts. Pick-partners. You don't get closer than that, down here.
The camp's called the Buried Seam now, they tell me. Back then we just called it home.
The shift was robbing the pillars. The ore in them was so pretty. Tamsin said the roof was singing wrong. Nobody listened.
The day the roof came down, I was up top fetching lamp oil. Tamsin was working my turn at the face. I've never forgiven the oil.
We dug for nine days. Then the Guild called it off and sealed the drift. I didn't go up with them.
Somebody ought to stay where they can hear them. So I stayed. That was the start of it.
I wandered a long while after. Months. Then I found this hollow, warm and green, and it didn't ask me to leave.
The first year I nearly starved. The second I learned mushrooms. The third I learned to talk to them.
Tamsin taught me to sound a roof. Tap, and listen, and tap again. I still sound this one every morning.
I stopped counting at a hundred shifts. Thirty years, I think. Maybe more.
Some nights I still hear picks, far off. Then I remember which way the Seam lies, and I put the kettle on.
My people up top will have stopped waiting long ago. That's all right. I'd want them to.
I went back to the Seam once. Lamps were moving in the rubble where no lamps should be. I've not been back.
Young Pell was on that shift. A first shift. So proud of that first lantern. Carried it like a newborn.
Is Old Hesk still alive? Hesk owes me six coppers and a story with an ending.

## hermit.work
Mushrooms first thing. Turn the logs, mist the beds, pick what's ready. Say good morning to each one.
Night-sight brew: water, a pinch of the red wart, then a golden carrot. Gold I can dig. Carrots I have to beg.
I brew night-sight for anyone who passes. Delvers, mostly. Once a warden who'd dropped a torch in a flooded hall.
I had the red wart off a peddler, long ago, with a sack of its sour sand. It's never once stopped growing.
Every morning I sit by the pond with my palms flat on the floor and listen. The stone tells you if it means to move.
Gravel hisses before it slides. Stone creaks before it cracks. Water goes quiet before it breaks through. Listen for the quiet.
The pond wants tending. Pull the weed, feed Mudge, thin the lily pads so the light gets down.
A poppy in the stew and you'll see in the dark a while. A lily of the valley, and you'll wish you hadn't.
I keep the glow berries trimmed off the pond. They'd glow all the way to the bottom and keep Mudge awake.
Brewing is slow work. You watch the bubbles and you think. Mostly I think about mushrooms.
I mend my own boots, my own pots, my own roof. Everything down here I've mended at least once. Including me.
Bonemeal on a little mushroom and, if it likes you, up it goes, big as a hut. That's how the Bishop started.
The big ones need room. I dug the hollow wider, a shovelful a day, for two years, just for the Bishop.
I trade brews to the delvers for carrots and news. The carrots are usually better than the news.
Tap the roof before you trust it. Even a roof you've trusted thirty years. I tap mine every morning.

## hermit.adventure
Going exploring? Take more torches than you think, and twice as many as that.
If the stone underfoot turns black and soft and full of little lights, stop. That's the Hush. Turn round and tiptoe.
Something lives in the Hush that sees with its ears. Don't sneeze. Don't run. Don't even think loudly.
A night-sight brew is worth more than a lantern in a flooded cave. Take one of mine.
If you find the Buried Seam, don't go poking in the rubble. Let them sleep. Please.
Mind the roof. I mean it. Look up before you dig, every time.
The green caves, with the roots and berries? Safe as anything. Sit and eat a berry for me.
Follow water downhill and you'll find the deep. Follow it uphill and you'll find the way home.
The Old Delvers built down there, in black stone and gold. Lovely work. Lonely, now.

## hermit.joke
Why are mushrooms never lonely? They grow in rings of friends. ...Fairy rings. Never mind. The Bishop laughed.
I asked the Bishop for advice once. It said nothing. Wisest thing anyone's ever told me.
Old Thatch and I have the same trouble: mostly stem, and thin on top.
A bat flew into my stew once. I ate around it. Then it ate around me.
How do you find a hermit in the deep? Follow the smell of mushroom tea and the sound of someone losing an argument to a mushroom.
The delvers call me mad. I call them in for soup. We both come out ahead.
I'd tell you a joke about the roof, but it might bring the house down.
Mudge lives better than any king. Swims, eats, sleeps, smiles. Never once paid a tax.
What's a mushroom's favourite room? Any room without windows. Ha! That's all of mine.

## hermit.news
News? The mushrooms say rain up top. They swell when the surface is wet. Bring a cloak.
The bats came home early last night, all at once. Something's stirring out in the tunnels.
The pond went cloudy two days back. The stone above is shifting. It'll settle.
A delver came by last month. Says the Guild's opened a new camp. Same old faces, new holes.
The Bishop dropped a cap yesterday. First time in years. I don't know what it means. Probably nothing. Probably.
The drip from the roof has gone quicker. Spring up top, I'd guess. Or the stone is weeping. One or the other.
I heard the Watch drilling, far off through the stone. Clang, clang, shout. Comforting, somehow.

## hermit.heart
Do you know why I talk to the mushrooms? So I don't forget how words go. Silly, I know.
I don't regret staying. I regret the oil. I'll always regret the oil.
If Tamsin could see me now, there'd be laughing first. Then sitting down to stay a while.
Sometimes I put out two cups without thinking. Then I drink both. Waste not.
I'm not lonely. Well. A little. Visitors help. You help.
The quiet down here is gentle. Up top, the quiet was always waiting for something.
I'd like to see the stars once more. Just once. Then I'd come back down and tell the mushrooms all about them.
Stone gives, stone takes. It took a great deal from me. Then it gave me this hollow. I think we're square now.
There's a little lantern on my shelf that isn't mine. I brought it back from the Seam. I think it was young Pell's.
When my time comes, I'd like to lie under the Bishop. Good shade. Good company.

## hermit.rumor.none
The mushrooms aren't leaning any way at all. Nothing near, I'd say. Just stone.
I don't know of anything close by. I don't go far these days. My knees have opinions.
Nothing nearby, surface-walker. Just me, the Bishop, Old Thatch and Mudge. We're plenty.
The stone's quiet all round. That's either very good, or I'm going deaf.

## hermit.plata
Near the top, the stone has silver in it that moves. Silverfish. Break the wrong block and out they pour.
I found a husk in the stone once, silver and empty, curled like a moth's case. Long as I am tall. I left it be.
A traveller came down my crawl one autumn. Asked how many lived here, where I slept, which way the door faced. Never blinked once.
The delvers say the silver folk shed their skins like silverfish and walk about in new ones. I'd believe nearly anything, down here.
If a stranger ever brings me a gift and won't eat my stew, I'll know. Nobody refuses my stew. Well. Nearly nobody.

## hermit.night
At night I sit by the pond and watch the berries glow in the water. Two of every light. Twice the company.
Nights are when the stone talks most. Cooling, I think. Or remembering.
I bar the crawl at night with a bit of plank. Not that it'd stop much. It stops me worrying.
Zombies wander the tunnels at night, even up here. They don't come in. The mushrooms disagree with them.
Sleep here if you like, {player}. There's moss enough for two, and the Bishop keeps the drips off.
Mudge sleeps under the big leaf. I sleep under the Bishop. Everyone has a roof here.
