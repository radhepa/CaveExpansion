# Deepwatch wardens (two knights, two archers) of a Deepwatch Post, {place} (e.g. "Ironreach Post"), across a cave road.
# Speaker-neutral and gender-neutral: any of the four may say any line. The captain is {captain} or "the captain", never a pronoun.
# Pools the captain inherits from this file (greet.morning/evening/night, chat.new, chat.close, joke, rumor.none, plata,
# held.*, player.*) never mention the captain.
# Canon: motto "Below, we hold." (answer: "Above, they sleep."). Watch report: "Lamp lit, road watched, gate shut."
# Road challenge: "Who walks the road?" Answer: "One who means to walk back up." Oath: "I keep the lamp. I hold the road. I do not go down."
# The Deepgate: an Old Delver arch carved with their lantern mark (the Watch's white lantern banner was copied from it), shut with
# the Watch's iron door; its lever is locked and the captain carries the key. Behind it the Long Stair goes down further than any
# map, to "the Deep below the deep". Nobody passes without the captain's word. A knock from below: log it, never answer.
# The Long Patrol: years ago twelve wardens went down the Long Stair and five came back; the captain was the youngest of them and
# never says what is down there. Their whole Log entry: "The stair goes on." Time is kept by the watch-candle; the watches are
# the grey watch (morning), the lamp watch (evening), the dark watch (night). The Deep Dark is "the Hush", its hunter "the Listener".
# Shared with the miners: creepers are "hissers", skeletons "rattlers", surface folk "topsiders", the Plata Clan "the silver folk".

## warden.chat
The road below us runs on for miles. We only hold this stretch of it. That's enough.
Every watch, somebody writes a line in the Log. Even if the line is "nothing came".
Most days, "nothing came" is the best line in the Log.
The banner's a white lantern on black. The only lantern down here that never goes out.
Guild carts come up the road once a season with flour and arrows. We cheer them like a fair day.
You learn to sleep with one ear open. Some of us sleep with both open.
Hissers are the worst of it. You don't hear them coming. Just that hiss, and then you're praying.
Rattlers I don't mind. A skeleton comes at you honest, clattering all the way.
Spiders climb the walls and come over the top. That's why the crenels are set so close.
Cave spiders are small and nasty and they come in threes. Always carry milk for the bite.
The dripstone over the road drips all night. You stop hearing it after a month. Then you hear it in your dreams.
Never stand under a pointed dripstone. They fall, and they don't miss.
The brazier is the heart of the post. If it goes out, somebody's in trouble.
{captain} says the walls were here before any of us. The stones say they're older still.
The Watch is older than the Guild. Older than the village charters, some say.
I miss rain. You don't know you love rain until you've gone a season without it.
Bread goes hard down here in two days. We dunk it in the stew and call it a feast.
We bake in the mess oven once a week. That smell is the closest thing to home in these caves.
Don't touch the lever by the Deepgate. Not for a dare, not for a bet. Not ever.
People ask what's behind the Deepgate. A stair. The Long Stair. That's all I know, and all I want to.
The miners are good folk. Loud, though. You can hear a Guild camp a mile off.
When a delver goes missing, we're the ones who go looking. We don't always find them.
Torches every ten paces along the road. Leave a dark patch and something crawls out of it.
The archers keep score on the targets. The knights pretend not to care. They care.
Our walls aren't high. They don't need to be. Down here, nothing comes from the sky.
The Hush, we call the black places where the sculk grows. Down there, sound is all that matters.
Lava light is pretty, until you remember what it is.
If you hear water where there was no water yesterday, back away. Aquifers break through without asking.
A flooded hall is a drowned hall. Something always lives in the deep water.
I've counted every stone in the gatehouse arch. Two hundred and twelve. Long watches.
We chalk the names of the lost on the barracks wall. It's a long wall.
I write home when the Guild carts come. They carry the letters up. Half of them get there.
There's a tally by the gate of every hisser we've stopped short of the wall. It's a big tally.
{captain} went down the Long Stair once, with the Long Patrol. Twelve went. Five came back. Don't ask about it.
Years back, the Watch went to see if the hermit in the green caves was a monster. They came back fed and carrying mushrooms.
See the lantern carved over the Deepgate arch? Our banner was copied from it. The Old Delvers cut it long before us.

## warden.past
I joined the Watch at sixteen. Thought it'd be glory. It's mostly cold feet and counting.
My village had a mine. One shift, my father went down and didn't come up. So I came down to keep others coming up.
I trained at another post, further along the road. We lost it to a flood. The water just came.
Before the Watch I was a baker's apprentice. I still knead the bread here, when there's flour.
My first night on the wall, a hisser got close enough that I could hear it breathe. I didn't sleep for a week.
The oath is short. "I keep the lamp. I hold the road. I do not go down." I've never broken a word of it.
When I came, there were nine of us here. Now it's five. Most went home. Not all.
{captain} took me in when no other post would. I'd been sent off for sleeping on watch. Never again.
The year the aquifer broke, we fought drowned in the road for three days. The water was up to our knees.
I grew up topside, in a village by a river. Down here I dream about fish.
My first patrol, we found a lost delver sitting in the dark with no torch, humming. Took three days to get a word out.
We held this gate for a whole night once, against everything the deep road could send. The braziers never went out.

## warden.work
Four watches a day by the candle. Two on the wall, one at the gate, one asleep. We turn it round.
Every watch starts the same: check the lamp, walk the road, try the Deepgate bar. Lamp lit, road watched, gate shut.
Drills at every change of watch. Step, strike, step back. Again until it's dull. Dull keeps you alive.
I walk the road out to the last torch and back. If a torch is out, I find out why.
Hissers we take at range. Archers first, and nobody goes near until it's quiet.
Rule of the gate: nobody passes the Deepgate without the captain's word. Not you, not me, not a lord with a letter.
We sweep the fallen dripstone off the road every morning. A pointed stone underfoot will lame a warden.
We don't chase anything into the dark. We hold the light and let it come to us.
A delver comes up the road hurt, we patch them up. A delver comes up the road scared, we listen.
Webs across the road get burned the moment we see them. Spiders are patient builders.
Arrows get counted every watch. An archer who loses arrows buys the next round.
Anything that comes up the road gets met here, before it reaches the miners' camps. That's the whole job.
I log every sound I can't name. Most turn out to be gravel. Some don't.
A bucket of water by every brazier, a bucket of milk by every bed. Fire and poison, the two quick deaths.
The training dummies take a beating every day and never once complain. Best recruits we have.
If something knocks on the Deepgate from the other side, the rule is simple. Log it. Don't answer.
Fresh torches go up along the road every third day, whether the old ones need it or not.
Change of watch, we say it face to face: "Below, we hold." "Above, they sleep." Then the old watch can rest.
Each season one of us runs word to the Guild camps. "Cave roads quiet." The miners laugh. They're glad of it all the same.

## warden.adventure
Going down the road? Go as far as the last torch, then think hard.
If the stone turns black and soft and pulses under your feet, walk away. Slowly. Silently.
That's the Hush. Something lives there that hunts by sound. We call it the Listener. Don't let it hear you.
Diamonds lie deep, down in the deepslate. So does everything else that wants to eat you.
Take a water bucket for the lava, a pick for the walls, and twice the torches you think you'll need.
If you find a flooded hall, don't dive for treasure. Something down there is already swimming.
The purple caves that ring when you step? Geodes. Safe enough. Beautiful, too.
In the green caves with the hanging berries, the air is sweet and the pond-folk are friendly. Rest there.
Light behind you, light ahead. The Guild says it, and the Guild is right.
Mark your way with torches on one side only. Then you'll always know which way is back.
If you find anything carved with a lantern over crossed picks, that's the Old Delvers. Tread respectful.

## warden.joke
How many wardens does it take to light a torch? One to light it, three to say it's crooked.
A hisser walks up to the gate. I call, "Who walks the road?" It says, "Ssss." Worst answer we've had all year.
Why don't rattlers ever stand watch? No guts. I've told that one forty times. It still works.
The stew tonight is "mystery stew". The mystery is whether it was ever food.
Our archers can hit a spider at forty paces. Our cook can't hit the pot at two.
There's a slime down the road that comes up every few weeks. Same slime every time. We call it Old Wobble.
A zombie came up the road once in a delver's helmet. Best-dressed thing we'd seen all season.
What's the difference between a cave spider and a warden on the dark watch? The spider's awake.
Someone asked if it gets lonely down here. I said no, the bats visit. Constantly. They never leave.
I bet a miner my boots the roof wouldn't drip on me all night. Walked home barefoot.

## warden.heart
I'd give a month's pay to see a sunrise. Just one, from the top of a hill.
Some nights I think about walking up the road and never coming back. Then the candle burns down and I check the lamp.
My people up top don't really know what I do. They think I guard a door. I suppose I do.
The quiet isn't the hard part. It's what the quiet might be hiding.
We're not heroes. We're the reason nobody needs one.
{captain} carries more than the rest of us put together. I wish I could take some of it.
When I hear a miner laughing on the road, that's my pay. That's the whole of it.
I'm afraid of the Deepgate. There, I've said it. Everyone here is, and nobody says it.
I'm not afraid of the dark anymore. I'm afraid of whatever doesn't need light.
Below, we hold. Above, they sleep. I like knowing someone up there sleeps easy because of us.

The silver folk, the miners call them. Folk who shed their skins like silverfish. I thought it was a tale.

Funny, isn't it? It's always dark down here, but you still feel the night come on.
