# The miners of a Delvers' Camp. Three of them share this file, so any line may come from any of them: no fixed
# backstory, no gender, small personal details only. Guild delvers from the villages above, down for a "shift"
# (a season). Warm, wry, practical, a little superstitious. The surface is "up top"; surface folk are "topsiders".
# Greeting: "Light to you." Reply: "And the roof over you." Sayings: "Mind the roof", "Light behind you, light
# ahead", "Stone gives, stone takes", "Tap the roof before you trust it", "Never rob a pillar" (the rule the Guild
# wrote after the Buried Seam, whose shift cut the ore out of the very pillars that held their roof up).
# Customs: every delver keeps their "first lantern" (given by their first foreman); a crust of bread on the first
# prop each shift for luck; one ring on the rail for every cart sent up; every Guild camp leaves one lantern hook
# empty, "the empty hook", for the Buried Seam's lost shift. The foreman keeps the Ore Ledger (every cart, plus a
# column for close calls); "Ledger day" ends a shift. Grumble is the camp's squeaky old ore cart. Old Hesk is a
# retired delver up top, teller of tall tales and rumours. Creepers are "hissers", skeletons "rattlers"; the Deep
# Dark is "the Hush" and the thing that hunts there by sound is "the Listener". The Plata Clan are "the silver folk".
# Never give the foreman a pronoun. Pools the foreman inherits (greet.morning/evening/night, chat.new, chat.close,
# rumor.none, plata, held.*, player.*) never mention the foreman.

## miner.chat
You know how you tell a good delver? Count their fingers, then count their years.
First rule down here: mind the roof. Second rule: see the first rule.
Tap the roof before you trust it. A hollow knock means walk away.
Every delver keeps their first lantern. Mine's got a dent from the day I learned to duck.
We leave a crust of bread on the first prop each shift. For luck. Or for the rats. Someone's happy either way.
Grumble's the old ore cart. Squeaks like a goat at every bend. We'd miss it if it stopped.
A shift's a season down here. You go down in green and come up in gold.
Deepslate's tough as an old boot. Your arms will tell you when you've reached it.
Copper goes green if you leave it. So do I, if you leave me down here long enough.
Lava down deep, water up high, and the diamonds always closest to the lava. The rock has a sense of humour.
Never dig the floor you're standing on. You'd be amazed how many folk learn that only once.
If a wall starts weeping, there's water behind it. Back off before it comes through to say hello.
Ever heard a geode? Purple crystal that chimes when you walk past. Prettiest thing under the sky.
{foreman} counts every cart. I once watched {foreman} count one twice, to be sure the first count was honest.
Glow berries! Found a patch in the lush caves. They taste of sunlight, near enough.
There are little pink fellows in the lush pools. Axolotls. Be kind to them and they'll fight beside you in the water.
The Old Delvers set gold into their floors. Gold! In floors! We use ours to pay the baker.
Stone gives, stone takes. Mostly it takes your knees.
Torches on your right going in. Coming back, keep them on your left and you're home.
The rock talks. Creaks, drips, pebbles falling. Learn to listen and you'll live long.
I like the camp quiet. Picks and kettles. Not the deep quiet, mind. That's a different thing.
Dripstone hangs from the roof like teeth. Don't stand under it when something shakes.
Lanterns over torches, if you can get them. They don't gutter, and the place looks lived in.
Folk think mining's about strength. It's about patience. And strength. Mostly patience.
Redstone glows when you strike it, like it's annoyed. I understand the feeling.
Lapis goes to the Guild's ink-makers and the chapel glass. Pretty stuff. Heavy as a bad conscience.
The forge corner's for mending picks, not warming boots. Tell {foreman} I said that. I'll deny it.
Mushrooms grow in the dim spots. Worse things grow in the dark ones. Keep it lit.
No weather down here. No rain, no snow, no sun in your eyes. I miss all of it terribly.
Half the time the best ore's in the wall you just walked past. That's mining.
Coal's honest. You always know where you stand with coal.
Lean on a prop, by all means. Just never kick one.
Gold's soft. Pretty, but soft. Like a merchant's handshake.
A tunnel two wide feels like a road. One wide feels like a coffin. We dig them two wide.
We name the drifts. There's Long Meg, the Elbow, and the one we don't talk about.
A good shift ends with everyone at supper and nothing to tell. The bad ones make better stories.
You've good hands for this. Rough enough, and all ten still on.

## miner.chat.new
First long stretch underground? You'll get used to the dark. You'll never like it.
Here's a Guild saying to start you off: light behind you, light ahead.
We're the Delvers' Guild. We dig, we haul, we go home. In that order, if we're lucky.
You'll want the rules. Mind the roof. Never rob a pillar. Share your stew.
If you hear a hiss, don't look. Run. Look later.
Ask me anything. Except where the good seam is. Everybody asks that.
Folk up top think we live in the dark. We don't. We live in a very small piece of light.

## miner.chat.close

I don't say this much, {player}, but it's better down here with you around.
If I ever don't come up, tell the folk up top I went happy. Or at least well fed.

You're Guild now, whatever the Guildhall says. Down here, that's what counts.
I worry about you, out there alone in the dark. Light behind you, light ahead. Promise me.

## miner.past
My first shift, I was so scared I lit nine torches in a tunnel four paces long.
My grandmother dug copper before the Guild had a hall. Said she could smell a good seam.
I'm a farmer's child. Turned out I liked digging, just not waiting for rain.
Took my Guild oath with a lantern in one hand and a pick in the other. Then dropped both.
The Guildhall's long table has every member's mark cut into it. Mine's crooked. I was nervous.
I've worked more shifts than I can count on one hand. Two hands, if I'm honest. I'm not always honest.
Before the Guild I hauled stone for a mason. Same rock, less dark, worse pay.
My first foreman told me: the rock doesn't hate you. It doesn't love you either. Remember that.
I learned to sound a roof from an old delver who was half deaf. Felt it through the haft, they said.
I was cut off behind a fall once. Three days. Sang every song I knew, twice. The diggers asked me to stop.
I first came down to pay off a debt. Stayed because I liked it. Don't tell my creditor.
As an apprentice I carried the water bucket a whole shift. Taught me where water hides.
I met {foreman} on my second shift. Got shouted at for leaning on a prop. Haven't leaned since.
My first gold, I spent on a new lantern and a very bad hat.

## miner.night
Can't sleep. The rock's settling. It sounds like someone turning over in bed.
At night I think about the sky. All that air with nothing holding it up. Terrifying.
Only the coals for light now. I like them. They don't ask anything of you.
Some nights, far below, you can almost hear the Hush. A quiet that has a sound to it.
I dream of rain on a roof. A wooden roof. With sky on top of it.
The empty hook over the door. Every Guild camp has one. At night I look at it longer.
If you hear knocking in the walls at night, it's water. Probably. Go back to sleep.

## miner.work
Today's face is iron with a copper streak. Good honest rock.
Prop every three paces. Four if the rock's sound. Five if you're a fool.
Never rob a pillar. The ore in it is holding up the ore above you.
Deepslate wears a pick twice as fast. Keep a spare, and keep both sharp.
We cut the seam, sort the ore, cart it out, and {foreman} writes it in the Ledger. Then we do it again.
A dull pick goes to the grindstone, not back to the wall. Dull tools make careless delvers.
Gravel in the roof? Get out from under it. Gravel doesn't wait to be asked.
Sand and gravel fall. Stone stays. Learn which is which before you need to.
Water seeping from the face means a buried lake close by. Plug it, mark it, move on.
When you break into a cave, stop. Listen. Then light it. Then step in.
If the wall's warm, there's lava behind it. Don't find out how much.
Raw iron in the bins, coal in the scuttle, copper in the cart. Gold goes to {foreman} and nobody else.
I've twelve carts to fill this week. I'm at nine and a half. Grumble lost the half on a bend.
Rails get checked every morning. A cart off the rails is a cart on your foot.
Lantern at the face, lantern at the turn, lantern at the shaft. Light behind you, light ahead.
Strip-mining's honest work, but dull. I sing to the rock. It hasn't sung back. Yet.
We dig in pairs. One swings, one watches the roof. Swap often, or you both stop seeing it.
Diamonds sit deep, near the lava, in the hard grey stone. Worth the trip. Not worth dying for.
Every cart that goes up, we ring the rail once. The whole camp hears it and knows we're working.
The ladder shaft's the way up we use. Never climb it with full arms. Ask me how I know.

## miner.adventure
Going deeper? Take more torches than you think. Then take more than that.
Long tunnels mean rattlers. Skeletons, topsiders call them. Meet them round a corner, never down a straight.
If the stone goes black and the moss looks like it's breathing, you've found the Hush. Turn round. Quietly.
In the Hush, walk soft and say nothing. Something down there hunts by sound. We call it the Listener.
Cave spiders are small, blue, and quick. Never break a cobweb you can walk around.
Hissers, the green ones: if you hear the hiss, you've a heartbeat left. Spend it running.
Carry a bucket of water. Puts out fire, gets you down a cliff, and turns lava to stone.
Mark your way back. Torches on one side, always the same side. The dark all looks alike.
If you find Old Delver stonework, black tile and gold, go gently. They built things to last, and things to keep out.
Slimes bounce about in the low wet caves. Annoying, not deadly. Unless you're near a drop.
Eat before you go down, not when you're hungry. Hunger makes a fool of you in the dark.
The Deepwatch posts are friends to anyone honest. Show them you're no trouble and they'll share their fire.

## miner.joke
What did the hisser say to the delver? Nobody knows. Nobody ever hears the end of that conversation.
How many delvers does it take to light a lantern? Three. One to light it, two to argue whose turn it was.
I asked the rock how it was doing. Nothing. Stone-faced, as usual.
Why's the Deepwatch so serious? You'd be too, if your best friend was a wall.
What do you call a delver who digs straight down? A short story.
My pick and I have an understanding. I swing, it complains, and the wall pays.
I'm not saying the stew's thin, but I saw my reflection in it. And a pebble.
Copper's the only thing down here that ages worse than me.
A topsider asked if the dark bothers me. I said, what dark? I'd lit forty torches.
Zombies love delvers. Well. They love the taste.
You can always tell a delver's been at your table. The bread's gone and there's gravel in the butter.
I've been down so long my knees creak in the same key as the roof.
Grumble squeaks, the roof creaks, and {foreman} sighs. Together, it's nearly a song.

## miner.heart
My mother sends a letter down every new moon. Same words each time: eat, sleep, come home. I manage two of three.
There's someone up top who waits for me at the end of every shift. That's who the lantern's really for.
I miss the smell of cut hay. Down here everything smells of stone and other people's socks.
My little brother wants to join the Guild. I want him to be a baker. We'll see who wins.
I keep a pressed flower in my helmet. From a meadow I've only seen twice.
Sometimes I'm afraid. Not of the dark. Of coming up and finding the village went on without me.
After a whole shift down, the first sunlight makes you weep. Everyone blames the brightness.
{foreman}'s gruff, but would walk into a rockfall for any of us. I know it.
I think of the Buried Seam more than I let on. Every delver does.
I've a friend up top who never writes back. I write anyway. One day they'll answer, or I'll go and knock.

## miner.plata
Near the surface we sometimes break into a silver husk in the stone. Empty. Shaped like a person. I don't like it.
Old Hesk tells of folk who shed their skins like silverfish. Silver folk. I laughed. Then I found a husk.
If a stranger on the road won't eat with you, won't shake your hand, and won't meet your eye, walk on. That's all.
Silverfish in the stone are bad enough. If the tales are true, some of them walk on two legs.
A traveller came down once, asking too many questions about our shaft. Left no footprints in the dust. I'm sure of it.
Smooth grey stone near the top that breaks too easy? Leave it. Something might be sleeping in it.
The silver folk? A tale for children. So why does every old delver tap a stone twice before breaking it?
